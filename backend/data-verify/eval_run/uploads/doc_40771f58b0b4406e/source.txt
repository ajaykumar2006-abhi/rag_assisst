HELIOS DEEP FIELD SURVEY - MISSION OVERVIEW

Helios Deep Field Survey is a fictional robotic observatory mission operated by the Cartographer Consortium. The mission launched on 14 March 2031 from the Kourou equatorial range and entered its science orbit on 2 August 2031 after a 141 day cruise.

The primary science objective is a wide-field survey of the Norma Cluster, a concentration of galaxies at roughly 70 megaparsecs from Earth. The survey is designed to catalogue low surface brightness structures that earlier wide-field instruments resolved poorly.

The spacecraft carries three instruments: the Wide Field Imager, the Low Frequency Spectrometer, and the Fine Guidance Sensor. The observatory operates in a low Earth orbit at an altitude of 612 kilometres with an inclination of 28.5 degrees.

Mission operations are distributed across three ground stations. The primary control centre is at Svalbard, with a backup tracking facility in Punta Arenas and a data downlink station on Ascension Island. The mission is designed for a five year prime phase with a three year extended phase contingent on propellant margin.

Total mission cost through the prime phase was 418 million euros. The consortium comprises nine member institutes across six countries. Data products are released to the public archive after a proprietary period of twelve months.
